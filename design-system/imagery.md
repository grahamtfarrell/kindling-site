# Imagery

Kindling photography looks like a founder's own film roll: warm, grainy and unposed. Use it for every product and story image; the 11 supplied photographs are the reference set.

## What the photographs share

- **Light and colour.** Low golden-hour or window light, warm shadows, and a saturated orange, ochre or red set against a complement (blue sky, teal water, dark green). The palette of the site is picked from the pictures: `orange`, `amber`, `olive`.
- **Subject.** Real people doing something, seen from behind, side or below, rarely facing the lens: a hand choosing fruit, a person at a desk, a swimmer mid-splash, a reach toward flowers. Objects and places can stand alone when they carry the palette (a copper car, a water surface, a blurred daisy).
- **Camera.** Film grain, slightly soft focus or motion blur, off-centre crops, portrait orientation (3:4 to 4:5). One panoramic motion-blur image is used full bleed with white text on top.
- **Avoid.** Stock-office handshakes, laptops on white desks, cold blue-grey grading, symmetrical studio portraits, visible logos.

## Treatment

- Frame in `PhotoFrame`: square corners, no border on filled images, optional chamfered top-right corner.
- Stamp the four-line Roboto Mono caption in `white` at bottom-left, 16px in from the edges; keep it clear of faces.
- Number images in a series with the marker row above the frame (`► 01 … ►`).
- For text over a photograph (hero, `ProcessRail`), check white against the darkest 4.5:1 region behind it or lay an `ink` scrim.

## Making new images in this style

The supplied set is small (about 200px wide) and needs to be replaced with full-resolution originals for production. To make proprietary images in the same style, brief a photographer or a generator with: "35mm colour film, golden-hour sun, fine grain, warm shadows, saturated orange against a blue or green complement, candid person absorbed in a task, seen from behind or in profile, off-centre composition, portrait 4:5, no text, no logos." Review every generated image against the reference set for palette match, then add the caption overlay in code rather than baking it into the image.
